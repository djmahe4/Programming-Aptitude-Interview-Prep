# **90-Day Cybersecurity Roadmap for Beginners**  
[*(Based on the video “FASTEST way to become a Cyber security engineer as a beginner (90 day plan)” by The Social Dork | Cyber Security)*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z1uZED00cFg)

This structured 3-month plan is designed for absolute beginners who want to build foundational knowledge, gain practical experience, specialize, incorporate AI skills, and position themselves for entry-level cybersecurity roles. The creator emphasizes that rushing is not required—take longer if needed—but following the sequence builds strong fundamentals and portfolio evidence. Cybersecurity demand remains high due to the digital nature of modern systems and the growing importance of protecting AI-driven environments.

### Why Cybersecurity?
Everything is digital, creating constant opportunities for malicious actors. Professionals are needed to safeguard systems, especially as AI expands the attack surface and the need for protection. The field is stable and growing; entry is realistic with focused effort, hands-on work, and patience.

**Key principles from the video:**
- Do not obsess over memorizing every definition (even experienced professionals look things up).
- Prioritize foundations (networking, operating systems, databases) before specialization.
- Build and document home labs—these become powerful resume and interview assets.
- Choose a path (offensive/red team, defensive/blue team/SOC, or GRC) after foundations.
- Add AI security concepts for modern relevance.
- Stay patient: first roles can take time; consistency beats switching plans repeatedly.

### Month 1 / Step 1: Foundations
**Focus:** Build core knowledge across cybersecurity fundamentals.

**Primary action:**
- Complete the **Google Cybersecurity Professional Certificate** on Coursera.  
  Covers nine domains including networking, threats, Linux/operating systems, vulnerabilities, and Python automation for security tasks. This is the recommended starting point.

**Strongly recommended additions:**
- Optionally complete **TryHackMe SEC1** (or Cyber Security 101 path) in parallel for hands-on labs.
- Study the **CompTIA Security+** course material (e.g., free Professor Messer YouTube series or Zero to Mastery course). Even if you do not sit the exam immediately, the knowledge strengthens foundations and is recruiter-friendly. List the course completion on your resume.

**Notes:**  
These cover the three essential domains the creator stresses: networking, operating systems, and databases. Avoid trying to master everything perfectly in one pass.

**Resources mentioned:**
- Coursera Google Cybersecurity Certificate
- [TryHackMe SEC1](https://tryhackme.com/socialsec1)
- CompTIA Security+ materials (Professor Messer free videos or structured courses)

### Month 2 / Step 2: Specialization + Hands-On Labs
**Focus:** Go deeper into a chosen domain and build real practical experience.

**Specialization via TryHackMe:**
- After foundations, decide direction:
  - **Offensive / Red Team / Penetration Testing** → TryHackMe PT1 (Penetration Tester) certification and learning path.
  - **Defensive / Blue Team / SOC Analyst** → TryHackMe SAL1 (Security Analyst Level 1) or similar.
- Paths are 100% hands-on, browser-based labs. Only a laptop + internet + TryHackMe subscription is required. Discount links for exams (SAL1, SAL2, junior pentest certs) are often available.

**Build four key home labs** (document everything thoroughly for resume/interviews):
1. **SIEM Lab** – Deploy Wazuh (open-source). Learn to read logs, summarize alerts, and understand SOC workflows.
2. **Web Application Firewall (WAF) Lab** – Deploy a WAF; pair with a Kali Linux attacker VM to practice both offensive attacks on a vulnerable web app and defensive protection.
3. **AI-based SOC Analyst Lab** – Integrate AI, APIs, API keys, and Python for modern detection and analysis skills.
4. **Honeypot Lab with Attack Dashboard** – Use OpenCanary to deploy fake services (SSH, HTTP, SMTP, FTP, etc.). Attack from Kali and observe logs in your SIEM. This extends the SIEM lab.

**Alternative non-technical path:**
- If you prefer less hands-on technical work: **Governance, Risk, and Compliance (GRC)**.  
  Recommendation: GRC Mastery course by Abed Hamdan (The Unix Guy). Foundations are still required, but deep tooling experience is less critical.

### Month 3 / Step 3: AI Security Skills + Job Readiness
**Focus:** Modernize your profile with AI security knowledge and prepare application materials.

**AI Security:**
- Complete **TryHackMe AI1** course.  
  Covers critical modern topics: RAG security, data poisoning, AI threat modeling, AI supply chain security, and related concepts. These are increasingly expected for cybersecurity engineers.

**Resume, Portfolio & Job Search:**
- Use the free beginner cybersecurity resume template provided in the video description. Structure it to highlight:
  - Projects / home labs (with documentation and links where possible)
  - Skills section
  - Relevant certifications/courses
  - Links (GitHub, LinkedIn, lab write-ups, etc.)
- Search LinkedIn for “cyber security” roles—plenty of openings exist.
- Apply strategically. Document labs and projects clearly so they stand out in interviews.
- Network: Connect on LinkedIn, engage with the community, and ask questions.

**Mindset reminders:**
- Do not abandon the plan after a few months of applications without results.
- Progress at your own pace; 3 months is a guide, not a rigid deadline.
- Belief and consistency matter.

### Suggested Weekly Cadence (Flexible)
- **Month 1**: Heavy on structured courses + light lab exploration.
- **Month 2**: Mix specialization paths with dedicated lab-building sessions; document as you go.
- **Month 3**: AI course + intensive resume/portfolio polishing + applications (aim for consistent weekly applications).

Track progress in a simple notebook or digital checklist. Revisit basics as needed. Once you land an entry-level role (help desk → SOC, junior analyst, etc.), continue specializing.

This roadmap prioritizes practical, job-relevant skills over pure theory or random tool hopping. Follow it step-by-step, document your work, stay patient, and you will be well-positioned to break into cybersecurity. Good luck!
